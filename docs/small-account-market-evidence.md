# Market access, costs and empirical evidence

Researched 2026-09-30. Published prices are not execution guarantees; instrument permission, account tier, quote and minimum size must be verified at the point of use. This is research for the stated experiment, not a suitability assessment. No account-specific broker entitlement was available.

## Market comparison for £100

| Market | Size/cost mechanics | Execution, risk and access | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK cash equities | Fractional access depends on broker; many purchases incur 0.5% stamp tax before spreads/fees. Fixed commissions are large relative to £100. | Listed-session liquidity varies; opening auctions and small shares can gap. Fully paid cash has no financing charge. | Poor high-turnover candidate. |
| US cash equities | Fractional dealing can accommodate £100; GBP conversion can add costs on both legs. | Regular US session is 09:30–16:00 New York; auction/overnight execution differs. Cash settlement and account permissions restrict capital reuse; no assumption of unrestricted day-trading buying power. | Viable research with low turnover; no measured qualified edge here. |
| ETFs | Some UK ETF transactions qualify for stamp-tax exemption. Fund expenses and spread remain; distributions/adjustments matter. | US tickers in the catalog are research proxies, not automatically orderable by a UK retail account. UK CCI transition and broker product permissions require verification. | Most promising *infrastructure* direction: liquid, fractional, UK-accessible cash funds. Exact product transfer not validated. |
| Spot forex / rolling FX | Very small units at some dealers, but minimum tickets, spreads and financing vary. Not equivalent to commission-free currency conversion. | Generally weekday trading; OTC execution, gaps and leverage. Need dealer-specific minimum units and live bid/ask history. | No trade; no validated FX dataset/edge and £100 constrains sizing. |
| Crypto spot | Fractional units and 24/7 trading; Kraken published lowest-tier taker fee 0.80% each way, about 1.6% round trip before spread. Venue order size and quote-currency minima both apply. | GBP pairs avoid an explicit USD conversion leg; volatility, exchange custody and outage risk remain. No lending/funding assumed for owned spot. | Public data/paper integration only. Frequent small-target strategies face severe fee drag. |
| Listed futures | Indivisible contract plus broker/exchange/clearing fees and variable margin. MES is $5 × index, $1.25 per minimum tick. | Margin is not maximum loss; gap/liquidation risk. Near-continuous sessions do not eliminate weekend risk. | Not economically compatible with controlled £0.25–£0.50 planned risk on £100. No futures tested. |
| Options | Premium × contract multiplier, spreads and per-contract charges; cheap premium can still expire worthless. | Theta, implied volatility, assignment/exercise and discontinuous exposure. Naked short exposure is excluded. | Cheap contracts do not establish positive expectancy; no options data or qualified edge. |
| CFDs / spread bets | Minimum stake/contract is provider/instrument dependent; spread, financing, possibly commission/guaranteed-stop premium. | FCA retail CFD leverage limits 30:1 to 2:1, margin close-out and negative-balance protection. Protection against debt does not prevent losing the deposit. | Rejected for this account/target; leverage does not solve the missing edge. |
| Crypto derivatives | Funding, leveraged liquidation, basis and counterparty risk. | FCA ban on retail crypto derivatives remains despite permitted retail access to qualifying crypto ETNs. | Excluded; funding carry and perpetual strategies not deployed. |
| Prediction/event markets | Binary payoff, spread, queue/liquidity and event-resolution risks. | UK Gambling Commission says products meeting the gambling definition require its licence. Platform availability/licensing was not established for this account. | Excluded; no circumvention of geographic restrictions. |

## Cost arithmetic

Model cash-equity research at 15 bp FX per side + 2 bp total spread + 3 bp slippage per side: approximately **38 bp round trip**. This is a stated conservative proxy, not a verified XLE fill cost. A GBP-funded GBP-listed fund may avoid FX conversion, but is a different instrument requiring its own test. USD asset returns translated at constant FX are not actual GBP returns.

Model Kraken GBP spot at 80 bp taker per side + 10 bp total spread + 5 bp slippage per side: about **180 bp round trip**. The forward paper monitor uses observed spread and fresh venue minima. It retains conservative 80 bp fees regardless of possible subsequent volume discounts. No maker fills are assumed; a passive order may not fill and may be adversely selected.

For gross expected edge `g`, proportional round-trip cost `c`, fixed round-trip fee `F`, and allowed cost share `a`, minimum notional is `F / (a*g - c)` when the denominator is positive. Otherwise **no trade size repairs the cost problem**. With assumed 100 bp gross edge, 20 bp proportional cost, £1 fixed fees, and at most half the edge spent on costs, the minimum is £333.33—more than this account. With a 10 bp short-term edge and 38 bp proportional costs, no notional works. These are break-even examples, not discovered edges.

At 1% stop distance, 180 bp round-trip costs consume approximately 1.8R before any directional profit. At 0.5% account risk, planned loss budget is only £0.50. Research sizing reserves both entry/exit costs, and can reject otherwise tempting tiny stops. Financing/funding and borrow are exactly zero only because the implementation is fully paid long-only cash/spot; adding leverage requires a different cost model.

Tax: share/fund disposals outside wrappers and crypto disposals can create tax obligations; token exchanges can also be disposals. HMRC describes individual financial spread-bet gains as generally outside CGT in the ordinary case, but this is not universal tax advice or a reason to prefer leverage. Annual allowances, other income/gains and status are unknown; all reported P&L is pre-tax. [Crypto disposals](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets), [spread betting](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-gains-manual/cg56105).

## Evidence review: academic evidence versus deployment inference

| Phenomenon | Original/replicated evidence | Contradictions and transfer limits | Research decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-sectional momentum | Broad historical stock evidence; live institutional implementation evidence in Ross/Moskowitz/Israel/Serban. | Momentum can crash; institutional net evidence does not establish retail intraday edge. | Existing ETF rotation remains a baseline; do not inherit old DSR claims. |
| Time-series trend | Moskowitz/Ooi/Pedersen study 58 futures/forwards; later long-history work extends evidence across regimes. | Predominantly monthly horizons, diversified leveraged contracts; not proof for 20-day single-asset entries or 30-day 10× returns. Whipsaw and leverage matter. | Test simple MA/Donchian variants, not elaborate ML first. |
| Short-term reversal | Liquidity provision can create gross reversal predictability; implementation papers show why costs can overwhelm it. | Gross predictability need not be monetizable; spread, adverse selection and queue position are central. | RSI/Bollinger tests; selected XLE rule failed robustness/qualification. |
| Intraday/opening-range momentum | Gao et al. document US intraday predictability; related market studies explore replication. | First/last-half-hour result is not validation of every opening breakout. Quote latency and spread erase small effects. | Reuse earlier ORB/scalping framework/evidence; current stored discovery rejection not overridden. |
| Overnight versus intraday | Intraday literature separates overnight information from within-session moves. | Daily close-to-close alpha cannot be assigned to an intraday strategy; opening gaps need next-open execution. | New replay includes gaps; no claim of a standalone overnight anomaly replication. |
| Volume and order flow | Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov connect order-book events and short-interval price impact. | Much is contemporaneous impact, not a freely exploitable future predictor. Bars do not reconstruct the queue or trade direction. | Volume rule tested as a simple proxy; order-book/volume-delta strategies rejected for missing data/latency evidence. |
| Pairs/statistical arbitrage | Gatev/Goetzmann/Rouwenhorst provide classic pairs-trading evidence. | Borrow fees, changing relationships, two-leg slippage and formation-selection bias; £100 cannot support diversified short portfolios here. | Literature reviewed; no fabricated pairs backtest without borrow and synchronized quote data. |
| Event-driven | PEAD and monetary-event research describe post-information drift; recent work also finds rapid incorporation after announcements. | Point-in-time event timestamps/surprises and tradable quotes essential; historical revised calendars leak. | Gap-continuation proxy tested, explicitly not an earnings strategy; genuine event execution deferred. |
| Crypto momentum | Liu/Tsyvinski find time-series momentum and attention effects; later work identifies market/size/momentum factors. | Historical samples, venue differences, regime shifts and today's retail costs limit transfer. Funding carry adds unavailable derivatives/counterparty assumptions. | BTC/GBP and ETH/GBP spot rule tests at current fee stress; no crypto strategy qualified. |
| Volatility clustering/regimes | Empirical motivation for volatility-aware risk; a regime label describes state, not necessarily forecasts excess return. | Switching doubles selection opportunities; VIX/ADX/funding filters must add independent net value. | ATR sizing and conservative risk reduction; no optimized regime switch or arbitrary confidence score. |
| Machine learning | Existing repo has LightGBM features, dated stores and evaluation infrastructure. | Statistical-unit and sample-design concerns invalidate old confidence; limited labels and sparse sentiment undermine complexity. | No new ML deployed because no demonstrated independently tested incremental benefit. |
| Retail day trading | Barber/Lee/Liu/Odean study detailed Taiwan trading records and persistence in losing activity. | Population evidence is not a personal ruin probability and not a UK strategy backtest. | Strong reason to require proof of net edge rather than extrapolate trading anecdotes. |

Primary research: [implementing momentum](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Implementing-Momentum-What-Have-We-Learned), [time-series momentum data/paper description](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data), [century of trend evidence](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/AQR-JPM-Fall-2017.pdf), [momentum crashes](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf), [short-term signals and costs](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/To-Trade-or-Not-to-Trade-Informed-Trading-With-Short-Term-Signals-for-LongTerm-Investors), [Gao et al. publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.009), [order-book events](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402), [pairs trading](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7032), [monetary momentum](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24748), [post-announcement jumps, recent preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08962), [crypto returns](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877), [crypto factors](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882), [day-trader learning](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf), [DSR methodology](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

AQR work is empirical primary research with a practitioner/asset-manager affiliation, not independent assurance. Exchange/broker fee documents establish mechanics, not alpha. Recent preprints are not treated as settled replication. Practitioner ORB/RSI/YouTube claims were not used as probability estimates. Internet anecdotes supplied **no parameters or deployment evidence**. No claim is made that a historical anomaly survives at this account's current execution costs simply because a paper reports it.
